Learning Objectives & The Big Idea

Slide 2 of 9

1 Construct a Difference Table (Δ1, Δ2) to Classify:

Subtract consecutive terms step-by-step. Whichever difference row becomes constant tells us the degree of the formula:

1st Difference (Δ1) Constant → LINEAR
General form: un = an + b
2nd Difference (Δ2) Constant → QUADRATIC
General form: un = an2 + bn + c
3rd Difference (Δ3) Constant → CUBIC Next Lesson
General form: un = an3 + bn2 + cn + d

2 Why Use the Difference Method & How We Find un

Row Δ1 measures the rate of change between terms, while Row Δ2 measures how fast that change itself is changing.

If Linear (Δ1 constant)
Use Your Known Algebraic Method

Use un = u1 + (n − 1)d (or check with GDC Linear Regression).

If Quadratic (Δ2 constant)
Use the CASIO fx-CG50 GDC

Enter n into List 1 and un into List 2, then run Quadratic Regression (X2).

Worked Example 1 • Linear

Classifying & Finding a Linear Formula

Slide 4 of 9
Problem

Use a difference table to determine whether the sequence 5, 9, 13, 17, 21, … is linear or quadratic, then find its general formula un.

Step 1: Difference Table

Since Δ1 = 4 is already constant on the first row (and Δ2 = 0), this sequence is LINEAR (un = an + b).

Step 2: Find un (Algebra)

Tool:
• First term: u1 = 5
• Common difference: d = Δ1 = 4
un = u1 + (n − 1)d
un = 5 + (n − 1)(4)
un = 5 + 4n − 4
un = 4n + 1
Check (n = 5): u5 = 4(5) + 1 = 21 ✓
